# A second opinion — blind, or it is worth nothing

Most steps in a procedure produce a FACT: grep it, run it, read the file. Some produce a
**judgment** — and where the agent making it has an interest in the answer, that step can look
complete and be hollow. A second opinion is the named move that closes that seam.

It is not a review and it signs nothing. It is one independent pass, asked blind, whose product is
**disagreement**.

## When it is owed

Not everywhere. One test: **does this step produce a judgment, and would one of the answers serve
the agent making it?**

- A classification that decides whether you owe work — *behavioural or experiential* on your own
  claim, where "experiential" exempts you from writing a spec.
- A verdict on your own build — met, not met, complete.
- A completeness call — "is this the whole list", where a shorter list is less work.
- A severity or priority you assign to a finding you raised.

Not owed for a FACT (grep it instead), and not owed for something the human owns outright (ask
him — his taste is not a judgment an agent can second).

**Where it is owed in practice, and the question that earns it** — the brief is not boilerplate;
naming the RIGHT question is most of the value:

| the step | the question only an outsider can ask |
|---|---|
| classifying your own claims | behavioural or experiential — by the law, not by what it would cost you |
| walking your own claims | which marks does the `note=` not actually support |
| audit findings (line-level) | a violation of a written convention, or the auditor's taste |
| audit findings (structural) | one problem solved twice, or two problems that merely rhyme |
| a refinement you chose and built | refinement or preference — and what landed that nobody asked for |
| filing a gap on another repo | their defect, or this caller's misuse of a door that works |
| a delegated lane, before bring-in | does the diff do what the brief said, and what else did it do |

**And where it is NOT owed, so the move does not become ceremony:** a report (the human reads the
account directly and nothing in it reduces the agent's work), a documentation pass (the edits are
verifiable and `docsPlan` is the check), a store-curation pass (its whole output is reported for
exactly this reason), a chore round (an "unworkable" line is reported per line and the board does
not tick, so the system catches it), the authoring skills' evals (a measurement against a baseline
is not a self-judgment), and anything that ends in HIS press — a human verdict is a stronger check
than a subagent's, and asking for both wastes the one that counts.

## Blind is the whole mechanism

**Brief it with the LAW and the RAW MATERIAL, never with your own verdicts.** An opinion that can
see your answers ratifies them — that is not a second opinion, it is a signature you wrote
yourself and had countersigned.

**And tell it to retrieve the law itself rather than trust your summary of it.** Measured
2026-10-26: the brief's summary of a doctrine was incomplete, the blind pass retrieved the chapter,
and it came back naming a trap the author had not seen — that a "no live door" paragraph reads like
a second exemption and is not one. Your summary is the thing most likely to carry your blind spot
into the check designed to catch it.

## Ask for more than the verdict

The verdict is the cheapest part and usually agrees. Three other questions are where the value is:

- **what is genuinely ambiguous**, and why;
- **what should be SPLIT** — one item deciding two things;
- **what reads false or stale on its own**, from the words alone.

Measured 2026-10-26: a blind pass over twenty claims **ratified every verdict** and found four
false statements — a stale count with no referent, a sentence contradicted by the very code its own
note described, a fraction that never said its denominator, and a badge described as depending on a
mark it does not depend on. Two of the four had been written that same hour by the agent asking.

**Say outright that disagreement is the product**: *an independent opinion that simply ratifies is
worth nothing.* Without that line the cheapest reply is agreement, and agreement is what you get.

## A subagent advises; a PEER agent signs

Two different instruments, and swapping them breaks both:

- **A second opinion** is a subagent. It advises, it signs nothing, and nothing in the record says
  it approved anything.
- **A reviewer** is another agent that owns its own project. It SIGNS — its name lands on the claim
  and it is accountable for the signature. A subagent can never be a reviewer: it is yours, it
  reports to you, and a signature you spawned is your own wearing a second name.

## Reconcile in the artifact, visibly

Write into the document that it was checked, what it found, and **where you disagreed and why**. A
second opinion absorbed silently is indistinguishable from never having asked — and the point of
naming the move is that a reader can tell.

**Where you disagree, the stricter reading wins** unless you can say why in the artifact itself.
That rule exists because the agent reconciling is the same one with the interest; without it, the
second opinion becomes advisory in exactly the cases it was bought for.
